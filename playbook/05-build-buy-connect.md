# 5. Build, buy or connect

> **Status: outline.**

## What this section decides

For each need, whether to buy a ready-made AI tool, connect existing systems to an AI platform the company already has, or build something custom, and how to test the options against each other instead of guessing.

## Write here

<!-- Aim for 500-800 words plus a decision table. A worked example of comparing options on cost and quality makes this section concrete. -->

## Questions to answer

- What are the three routes in practice (for example a licensed copilot, a connection through MCP, a connector or an API, a custom application)?
- Which criteria decide between them: fit with the work, quality, running cost, maintenance, security, vendor lock-in, time to value?
- When is a plain, deterministic automation better than AI at all?
- How do you test the realistic options side by side before choosing? What do you measure (quality on a fixed test set, cost per finished task, response time)?
- Why is cost per finished task a better measure than price per token?
- Who maintains what you build or connect, and what does that cost each year?
- How do you avoid being locked in? What is the exit plan for each vendor?
- How does security review fit in (data access, permissions, least privilege)?

## Must include

- [ ] A decision table: route, when to choose it, typical cost, typical risk
- [ ] The testing method for comparing options, with what is measured
- [ ] When not to use AI
- [ ] Maintenance and exit considerations

## Questions a sceptical leadership team will ask

- "We already pay for Copilot. Why would we need anything else?"
- "Who fixes this when the person who built it leaves?"
- "What will this cost us per year, not just to set up?"
